The most reliable way to improve typing speed is to build accurate movement first. If you repeatedly press the wrong key, practicing faster can make that mistake habitual. Start at a pace where you can read the text, recognize each next key and keep errors low. Increase speed in small steps only after a few stable sessions.

Choose one layout for each practice goal. A learner may know both Preeti and Unicode, but switching mappings in the middle of a focused practice block makes it harder to develop automatic recall. Set the layout before you begin and use the same setting when comparing results on different days.

Mix drills and real text. Individual letters expose weak key positions, while words and sentences teach transitions, matras and punctuation. When you notice a repeated error, isolate the troublesome combination for a minute, then return to a complete passage. Deliberate correction is more useful than repeating a full test while making the same mistake.

Use timed sessions to prepare for pressure, but keep a baseline without a timer too. A one-minute test is useful for a quick check; a five-minute run better reveals whether you can sustain focus. Record duration, layout, speed and accuracy so you can compare like with like. Take short breaks to avoid fatigue and tension.

Do not judge progress from a single attempt. A passage with familiar words can produce a higher number than new vocabulary, and a tired session may be slower even when your skill has improved. Review results over multiple days and choose one small objective for the next practice block. Relax your hands, sit at a comfortable height and stop if you feel pain; speed is not worth developing tension or an injury.

Good technique includes more than finger speed. Keep your hands relaxed, sit so that your shoulders do not rise, and place the screen where you can read the target without leaning forward. Pause briefly between rounds and stop if you feel pain. A comfortable setup supports longer practice and makes it easier to focus on accuracy. Keep a note of which characters caused errors, then begin the next session with those characters before taking another timed test. This makes improvement deliberate instead of relying on repeated random attempts.
